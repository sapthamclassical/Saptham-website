### Art Direction Guide & Visual Identity System · v1.0
**Saptham — Classical Music & Dance Club, College of Engineering Guindy (CEG), Anna University**

Every future design, animation, AI-generated asset, 3D model, illustration, motion graphic, photograph, and page must adhere to what is written here. ## THE TWO PILLARS (Read First)

Before any section, understand the two structural ideas that everything else is built on. These are not decoration — they are the load-bearing concept.

**Pillar 1 — SAPTA SWARA (The Seven Notes).**
The word *Saptham* derives from *sapta* (seven). The seven swaras — **Sa · Ri · Ga · Ma · Pa · Da · Ni** (Shadja, Rishabha, Gandhara, Madhyama, Panchama, Dhaivata, Nishada) — are the atoms of all Indian classical music. They become our **chromatic system**: seven signature hues, seven narrative movements, a spacing rhythm, a wayfinding spectrum. This gives the brand a rigorous, authentic skeleton instead of arbitrary "Indian gold" pastiche. Seven is our number. Own it everywhere: seven office bearers featured per row, seven-second ambient loops, a seven-point navigation.

**Pillar 2 — THE MARGAM (The Concert Order).**
A Bharatanatyam recital follows a fixed emotional arc — *Pushpanjali → Alarippu → Jatiswaram → Shabdam → Varnam → Padam → Thillana → Mangalam* — moving from invocation, to pure rhythm, to storytelling, to devotional depth, to dazzling finale, to blessing. **The website's scroll journey IS a Margam.** Arrival is invocation; the middle builds rhythm and story; the climax dazzles; the exit blesses and lingers. This is our narrative spine (see §4).

Together: **Swara = the system. Margam = the story.** Hold both in your head for every decision.

## 1 · BRAND VISION

**Vision Statement**
> To render Indian classical arts as a living, luminous, contemporary experience — a digital sabha where the discipline of the ancients and the precision of the modern web meet, so that a first-year student, a returning alumnus, and a global cultural patron each feel they have witnessed something sacred, current, and unforgettable.

**Positioning Statement**
> For the students, alumni, and admirers of Indian classical arts at CEG, **Saptham** is the digital concert hall where heritage is not preserved behind glass but performed in real time. Unlike a conventional college-club website that lists activities, Saptham's site is a cinematic, scroll-driven recital — because classical art was never meant to be catalogued; it was meant to be *experienced*.

**The One-Line North Star**
> *"Where Tradition Meets Technology"* — expressed not as a tagline pasted on a hero, but as a felt tension in every choice: the calligraphic serif against the machined grid, the oil-lamp glow rendered in WebGL, the kolam drawn by an algorithm.

**What success looks like**
- A visitor forgets they are on a "college club website" within five seconds.
- An alumnus feels a lump in the throat.
- A sponsor perceives prestige worth associating with.
- A prospective member thinks: *I want to belong to this.*
- A designer thinks: *this belongs on Awwwards.*

## 2 · BRAND PERSONALITY

**Primary Archetype — The Maestro** (a fusion of *The Sage* and *The Artist/Creator*).
Saptham is the guru who has mastered the tradition so completely that they can now bend it toward the new — authoritative but never arrogant, disciplined but deeply feeling. Wisdom expressed through beauty.

**Secondary Archetype — The Devotee.** There is reverence under everything. The brand bows before it performs.

**Brand-as-Person**
Imagine a woman in her late twenties: a trained Bharatanatyam dancer who is also a software engineer. She wears a single antique-gold temple jewel over minimalist black. She speaks softly, precisely, in both Tamil and English. She can discuss a raga's microtones and a shader's easing curve in the same breath. She is not loud. She does not need to be. When she performs, the room goes silent.

| Trait | We ARE | We are NOT |
|---|---|---|
| Tone | Reverent, assured, intimate | Loud, gaudy, "festival flyer" |
| Craft | Precise, hand-finished, deliberate | Templated, default, rushed |
| Emotion | Deep, restrained, then overwhelming | Sentimental, saccharine, kitsch |
| Modernity | Quietly cutting-edge | Trend-chasing, gimmicky |
| Heritage | Authentic, specific, sourced | Generic "ethnic," touristy |
| Luxury | Understated, material, earned | Flashy, glittery, ostentatious |

**Voice of Voice (microcopy)**
- Speaks in short, weighted lines. Uses white space like a rest in music.
- Bilingual by design: Tamil and English hold equal dignity. Tamil is never a "translation afterthought."
- Uses the vocabulary of the art form correctly (*raga, tala, mudra, abhinaya, margam, sabha, arangetram*) — with quiet glossing, never explaining down to the reader.
- Example eyebrow labels: `SA · THE FIRST NOTE`, `ARANGETRAM — THE DEBUT`, `எங்கள் பாரம்பரியம் / OUR LEGACY`.

## 4 · CORE EMOTIONAL JOURNEY

The visitor moves through the **Margam**. Each scroll-movement has a target emotion, a tempo, and a sensory register. This is the master map every page and animation must serve.

| Movement | Site moment | Emotion to induce | Tempo | Sensory register |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0 · Nada (The Hush)** | First 800ms of load | Held breath. Anticipation. "Something is about to begin." | Silence → a single struck note | Black screen, one point of gold light kindling like a lamp being lit. A faint tambura drone fades in. |
| **1 · Pushpanjali (Invocation)** | Hero | Awe, reverence, arrival | Slow *aalap* — unmetered, breathing | The name blooms in gold; a kolam draws itself; particles of ash/light drift. |
| **2 · Alarippu (Pure Rhythm)** | Intro / "Who we are" | Curiosity awakening, quickening pulse | First tala enters — measured, geometric | Type and grid resolve with rhythmic precision. Structure reveals itself. |
| **3 · Jatiswaram (Melody & Form)** | Vision / Disciplines | Wonder, aesthetic pleasure | Flowing, melodic, mid-tempo | Instruments and dance forms glide in; jewel colors appear one per swara. |
| **4 · Shabdam (The First Story)** | Legacy / History timeline | Pride, warmth, rootedness | Narrative, storytelling cadence | A vertical scroll through years like a temple gopuram tier by tier. |
| **5 · Varnam (The Centrepiece)** | Events / Signature productions | Immersion, transportation, the peak | The longest, richest movement — climb and climax | Full cinematic mode: near-full-bleed imagery, spotlit, sound-reactive. This is the heart. |
| **6 · Padam (Intimacy)** | Alumni & Board / Testimonials | Tenderness, connection, nostalgia | Slow, expressive, *abhinaya* | Faces, voices, quotes. Closer crops. The eyes lead. |
| **7 · Thillana (The Dazzle)** | Join / Call to belong | Exhilaration, desire, momentum | Fast, virtuosic finale | Bright, kinetic, ghungroo-quick micro-motion. "Come, be part of this." |
| **8 · Mangalam (The Blessing)** | Footer / Exit | Peace, gratitude, resonance | Ritardando — slowing to rest | Warm fade, a final gold glyph, the drone resolves to silence. They leave *moved*. |

**The rule of the afterglow:** the last emotion must be *resonance* — the feeling that lingers after the last note, when the hall is quiet but the air still hums. Design the footer and exit for that hum, not for "links."

## 5 · DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Seven principles. (Of course seven.)

1. **Darkness is the stage.** Our default is deep, warm near-black — the unlit sabha, the temple's inner sanctum. Light is precious and directed. We do not fill space with color; we *reveal* with light.
2. **Gold is light, not decoration.** Gold never sits flat as "a gold thing." It behaves like metal catching a flame — it has a source, a highlight, a shadow, a gradient. If gold isn't reacting to light, it's wrong.
3. **Space is silence.** Negative space is a musical rest. Generous emptiness is confidence. Crowding is panic. When in doubt, remove.
4. **The grid is the tambura drone.** Beneath every composition runs a steady, invisible order (the layout grid, the baseline, the 8-point rhythm) — the sruti against which every melodic element is tuned. Break the grid only the way a musician bends a note: intentionally, expressively, and back to true.
5. **Motion is raga.** Nothing snaps. Everything has attack, sustain, and decay. Time is the medium. (See §13.)
6. **Bilingual is respect, not a toggle.** Tamil and English are co-designed from the first pixel. Tamil is set with the same typographic love as Latin — never a machine-translated caption.
7. **Restraint is reverence.** The temptation with "Indian classical" is maximalism — every surface carved, every color saturated. We resist. One perfect gold line beats a hundred ornaments. Luxury is what we choose *not* to add.

## 6 · VISUAL LANGUAGE

**Shapes & Geometry**
The vocabulary is drawn from three authentic sources, abstracted to their geometric essence:

| Source | Primitive | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Gopuram / temple arch** | The pointed arch, the stepped pyramid (tiering) | Vertical hierarchy, section thresholds, image framing, scroll milestones |
| **Lotus / Ashtadala** | The circle and the eight-petal radial | Loaders, focal ornaments, avatar frames, section transitions |
| **Kolam pulli** | The dot-grid and the continuous unbroken line | Background matrix, dividers, generative motion, the layout logic itself |
| **Araimandi** | The equilateral triangle / diamond (the dancer's grounded stance) | Structural balance, card geometry, the "planted" feeling of blocks |
| **Alapadma mudra** | The opened palm / blooming ellipse | Reveal animations, hero bloom, "opening" gestures |

**Geometry rule:** Curves are calligraphic (they have thick-thin stress, like a brushstroke or a veena's neck), never uniform-weight tech-arcs. Straight lines are precise and architectural. The tension between *carved curve* and *machined line* is the whole brand in miniature.

**Spacing — the Tala Scale.**
Our spacing scale is tuned to rhythmic cycles (talas) instead of an arbitrary geometric ramp. Base unit = 8px (the sruti). Named steps:

| Step | Tala | Value | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tisra | 3 | 24px | Tight internal padding |
| Chatusra | 4 | 32px | Default component gap |
| Khanda | 5 | 40px | Related-group spacing |
| Misra | 7 | 56px | Sub-section spacing |
| Sankeerna | 9 | 72px | Section spacing |
| Aavartana | ×2 | 120–160px | Major movement breaks (the "breath" between margam sections) |

**Balance:** Asymmetric but weighted — like a stage where a single dancer stands off-center yet the composition is perfectly balanced by light and shadow. Avoid dead-center symmetry except for sacred/ceremonial moments (invocation, mandala loaders).

**Hierarchy:** Extreme contrast. One enormous gold serif headline; everything else recedes into quiet grotesque and mono. There is always exactly one "soloist" per view — the eye should never be confused about who is performing.

**Composition:** Cinematic. Think 2.39:1 letterbox crops, rule-of-thirds placement of the "soloist," deep foreground/background separation. Full-bleed for climactic moments (Varnam); framed and matted like a museum print for reverent moments (Padam).

**Negative space:** No section is "full." Aim for 40–60% intentional emptiness on editorial pages. The emptiness is the concert hall's darkness around the spotlight.

| Color group | Screen share | Note |

| Alta vermillion | **<1%** | A spark, never a field. |

## 8 · MATERIAL LANGUAGE

Objects on this site are not "flat UI." They imply substance. Each material has a defined role and a rule for restraint.

| Material | Looks like | Where it appears | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Temple Brass / Bronze** | Aged, warm, matte-to-satin metal with a soft anisotropic sheen; darkened in crevices | The gold line-work, dividers, key icons, instrument renders, the logo mark, CTA borders | Must always catch a directional light. Never chrome-shiny, never flat-yellow. Micro-scratches and patina = authenticity. |
| **Granite (temple stone)** | Dark, fine-grained, slightly speckled, honed not polished | Large background surfaces, footers, "foundation" blocks | Very low contrast grain (2–4% opacity texture). It is felt, not seen. |
| **Marble** | Cool-warm cream with soft grey-gold veining | Rare light "manuscript" sections, quote plinths, award/plaque callouts | Reserved for moments of prestige and pause. Veining subtle, never busy Carrara-drama. |
| **Silk (Kanjeevaram)** | Deep jewel-toned weave with zari (gold thread) shimmer; soft directional sheen | Hero gradient fields, section backdrops behind imagery, fabric folds in motion | The sheen shifts as you scroll (subtle gradient animation). The zari = fine gold specular lines. |
| **Carved Wood (sandalwood/rosewood)** | Warm brown with relief carving, soft shadow in grooves | Section thresholds, ornate frames for legacy/heritage content | Used at *edges and thresholds*, not as full backgrounds. Relief implies the hand-carved. |
| **Temple Stone (weathered)** | Ancient, sun-bleached, textured | Historical/legacy imagery treatment, timeline backgrounds | Signals *age and lineage*. Pair with warm low light. |
| **Gold Leaf (varak)** | Ultra-thin hammered gold with visible leaf-edges and micro-cracks | Precious highlights: the active nav state, a single glyph, "kintsugi" seams that trace kolam lines | The rarest material. One gold-leaf moment per page maximum. Its imperfection (cracks, torn edges) is the point. |
| **Clay / Terracotta (ghatam, diya)** | Earthy, matte, hand-thrown | Instrument (ghatam) renders, warm secondary surfaces | Grounds the palette; the humble counterpoint to gold. |

**The material philosophy:** Digital surfaces should feel like *objects in a candlelit vitrine* — you should almost want to touch them. But only ONE material dominates any given view. Layering brass + silk + marble + wood in one frame = a souvenir shop. Choose the material that matches the movement's emotion (silk for lush Varnam; marble for prestigious awards; granite for foundational legacy).

## 9 · LIGHTING DIRECTION

Lighting is arguably the most important craft dimension of this brand. The entire site is lit like a **single oil lamp (deepam) in a dark hall**.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Mood** | Chiaroscuro. Deep shadow, precious light. Reverent, intimate, cinematic. The feeling of a spotlight finding a single dancer in a black theatre. |
| **Temperature** | Warm. **2700K–3200K** key light (candle/lamp/incandescent). Optional cool fill at **4500–5000K** only to sculpt depth in shadows (moonlight through a mandapam). Never flat daylight, never blue-white LED. |
| **Key light** | Single dominant source, high and to one side (Rembrandt / 45°+). Creates one bright cheek/edge and a falling-off shadow. This is the "soloist" lighting. |
| **Fill** | Minimal. Let shadows go deep and rich (into warm black, not muddy grey). Contrast ratio high (4:1 to 8:1). |
| **Rim / edge light** | Warm gold rim on subjects and on brass — separates them from the black and makes metal *sing*. Essential on instruments and jewelry. |
| **Highlights** | Small, controlled, specular — the glint on a zari thread, a nose-ring, a struck cymbal. Never blown-out. The highlight is a jewel, so it must be small and perfect. |
| **Volumetric light** | God-rays / light shafts through faint haze — as if incense smoke drifts through a temple window. Used at movement thresholds and the hero. Subtle; suggests sacred atmosphere. |
| **Particles** | Slow-drifting motes: incense smoke, floating ash (vibhuti), fine gold dust, occasional jasmine petals. Density LOW — a few dozen, not a snowstorm. They must feel like air, not effects. |
| **Atmosphere** | A faint warm haze/bloom around light sources. Gentle vignette pulling focus to center. The air itself is warm and slightly thick, like a hall that has held music. |

**Lighting anti-patterns (forbidden):** flat even lighting; cold blue tech-glow; neon; hard flash (event-photography look); rainbow gradients; glassmorphism frost; garish HDR.

## 10 · TYPOGRAPHY

Type is where **"Tradition Meets Technology"** becomes literal. A high-contrast, hand-stressed **classical serif** (tradition, calligraphy, the human hand) is set in tension against a precise **neo-grotesque** and a **monospace** (technology, the grid, the machine). And Tamil is a first-class citizen.

### 10.1 The Typographic Cast

| Role | Recommended | Premium alternative | Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display / Headings** | **Fraunces** (variable; use high optical size, high contrast, slight "soft" axis) | **GT Sectra**, **Canela**, or **Ogg** | Literary, cinematic, high thick-thin contrast — reads as a printed concert programme cover. Set in Saptham Gold or Ivory. |
| **Secondary / Eyebrows & Labels** | **Geist** or **Inter** (tight tracking, uppercase, small) | **Söhne**, **Neue Haas Grotesk** | The machined counterpoint. All-caps, letter-spaced labels: `SA · THE FIRST NOTE`. The "technology" voice. |
| **Body** | **Newsreader** (for editorial serif long-form) or **Inter** (for UI/short text) | **Source Serif**, **Lyon Text** | Quiet, readable, warm. Serif for storytelling passages; sans for interface and metadata. |
| **Metadata / Technical** | **Geist Mono** or **IBM Plex Mono** | **JetBrains Mono** | The liner-notes/museum-placard voice. Dates, credits, raga names, coordinates, run-times. Adds editorial rigor + the "engineering" nod (this is CEG, after all). |
| **Tamil — Display** | **Noto Serif Tamil** | **Mukta Malar** (display weights) | Tamil headlines set with equal care; matches the serif's gravitas. |
| **Tamil — Body/UI** | **Catamaran** or **Hind Madurai** | **Noto Sans Tamil** | Clean, contemporary Tamil for interface and paragraphs. |

### 10.2 Type Scale (fluid; desktop reference)

| Token | Size / Line | Font | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Display XL | 96–160px / 1.0 | Fraunces | Hero title, movement titles ("VARNAM") |
| Display L | 64–88px / 1.05 | Fraunces | Section heroes |
| Heading | 36–48px / 1.15 | Fraunces | Sub-section titles |
| Title | 24–28px / 1.25 | Fraunces / Newsreader | Card & block titles |
| Eyebrow | 12–13px / 1.4, +0.18em, UPPERCASE | Geist/Inter | Labels, swara tags |
| Body L | 18–20px / 1.6 | Newsreader / Inter | Editorial paragraphs |
| Body | 16px / 1.6 | Inter | UI, standard copy |
| Mono meta | 12–13px / 1.5, +0.02em | Geist Mono | Dates, credits, technical |

### 10.3 Pairing & Usage Rules
- **One serif soloist per view.** The giant Fraunces headline is the star. Do not compete it with a second large serif.
- **Gold is for display, ivory is for reading.** Set headlines in gold or ivory; set body only in ivory/ash. Never body copy in gold.
- **Eyebrows are always mono or tracked-grotesque uppercase** — they are the "museum placard" that dignifies each section.
- **Tamil and English pairing:** when both appear, give Tamil equal size and weight, stacked or side-by-side, separated by a thin gold hairline — never Tamil as a smaller sub-caption.
- **Numerals:** use oldstyle figures in editorial serif contexts (they sit like jewelry in text); lining/tabular mono figures for data (years, counts).
- **Letter-spacing:** display serif = tight (−1% to −2%); eyebrows = wide (+18%); body = default. Tight display + wide eyebrow = the signature typographic rhythm.

## 11 · PHOTOGRAPHY DIRECTION

Every photograph must look like a **still from a cinematic performance film**, not an event snapshot. If it looks like it was taken with an on-camera flash at an auditorium, it is wrong.

| Dimension | Direction |
|---|---|
| **Camera angles** | Low, reverent angles looking slightly up at performers (heroic, temple-idol framing); intimate eye-level for abhinaya/faces; occasional top-down for kolam/feet/mandala compositions. Dynamic diagonals for movement. |
| **Lighting** | Chiaroscuro, single warm key (see §9). Rembrandt on faces. Deep shadow. Rim-light the silhouette and the jewelry. Practical lamps (deepam) in frame where possible. |
| **Framing** | Cinematic crops (2.39:1 and 3:2). Generous negative space (the dark hall) around the subject. The performer often off-center, balanced by shadow. |
| **Composition** | One soloist per frame. Foreground/background separation. Use architectural lines (pillars, arches) as framing devices. Rule of thirds; leading lines from mudras and gaze. |
| **Depth of field** | Shallow (f/1.4–f/2.8). The subject sharp, the hall melting into warm bokeh. Isolate a hand, an eye, a cymbal. |
| **Motion** | Embrace intentional motion blur — a spinning skirt, a whipping pallu, ghungroo-blur at the ankle — against a still, sharp core (the calm face, the planted foot). Stillness + motion in one frame = the essence of the dance. |
| **Backgrounds** | Deep, uncluttered — black theatre, a single carved pillar, a wash of jewel-toned silk, temple stone. Never busy stage clutter, banners, or plastic chairs. |
| **Human expression** | Truthful *abhinaya* — the trained, expressive face where the *eyes lead*. Focus, devotion, joy, transcendence. Show effort and sweat as authenticity, not glamour-retouch. The gaze is everything. |
| **Detail shots (essential library)** | Mudras (hands), alta-red feet mid-step, ghungroo/salangai, temple jewelry (jimikki, nethichutti, oddiyanam), the veena's yali head, fingers on strings, a struck mridangam, jasmine gajra, kohl-lined eyes. These macro/detail shots are the connective tissue between full performance images. |
| **Color grade** | Warm shadows, protected highlights, rich but not oversaturated jewel tones, crushed-to-warm-black blacks, a whisper of gold in the mid-tones. A unified "candlelit" LUT across all imagery. |

**Forbidden:** direct flash, cold white balance, cluttered stages, tourist saturation, stocky "diverse group smiling at camera," heavy beauty-retouch that erases effort, wide-angle distortion of faces.

## 12 · ILLUSTRATION STYLE

**Primary style: "The Living Line" — gold calligraphic line-art on darkness.**

- **Technique:** Single continuous or few-stroke line-work, drawn as if by a **kolam artist's hand** or a **Tanjore/Kerala mural outline** — but abstracted and minimal. Calligraphic weight (thick-thin stress), not uniform vector stroke.
- **Color:** Brass/gold line on Garbhagriha Black (default). Occasionally reversed (dark line on Chandana Sand) for manuscript sections. A single swara-hue may tint an illustration.
- **Finish:** Line-art may carry a subtle **gold-leaf/varak** fill or a fine engraved shimmer — "gold embossed" for hero moments. Flat-fill cartoon style is **forbidden**.
- **Subjects:** Instruments, mudras, deities-as-geometry (abstracted, respectful — never cartoonish gods), lotuses, yali, hamsa, peacock, gopuram silhouettes, dancers as flowing line.
- **Behavior:** Illustrations prefer to be **drawn on** (self-drawing stroke animation) on scroll — the line appears as if being written in real time. This is the "generative kolam" signature.

**Style ladder (when to use what):**
1. **Line-art (default)** — icons, spot illustrations, dividers, generative kolam.
2. **Gold-leaf relief** — hero/ceremonial focal illustrations (one per page max).
3. **Editorial engraving** — legacy/history sections (fine cross-hatch, old-manuscript feel).
4. **Silhouette** — dancers and instruments in atmospheric/photographic contexts (§16–17).

**Forbidden:** flat modern "corporate Memphis" illustration, cutesy mascots, gradient-blob 3D, clip-art gods, oversaturated calendar-art.

## 13 · MOTION LANGUAGE

> **The governing metaphor: motion is music.** Every animation has an *attack* (how it enters), a *sustain* (how it holds), and a *decay* (how it settles) — exactly like a plucked veena string or a struck mridangam. Nothing on this site "snaps."

**Motion philosophy**
- Motion serves *rasa* and *narrative*, never spectacle for its own sake.
- The site has a *tempo*. It opens *unmetered and slow* (aalap), builds through *tala*, climaxes, and *resolves*. Speed is emotional, not uniform.
- Motion is *directed by scroll* — the visitor is the conductor; scrolling is moving through time/performance.

**Speed & easing**

| Interaction type | Duration | Easing | Feel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro (hover, tap, focus) | 180–260ms | `cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1)` (soft overshoot) | A lightly plucked string |
| Element entrance | 600–900ms | `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` (expo-out, long decay) | A note swelling and settling |
| Section/movement transition | 900–1400ms | Custom "veena decay" (slow ease-out with a long tail) | A sustained tone fading |
| Ceremonial (hero bloom, invocation) | 1600–2800ms | Unmetered, breath-like ease-in-out | An unhurried aalap |
| Page transition | 800–1200ms | Curtain/light-fade ease | The house lights dimming |

**Signature behaviors**
- **The Kindling (load):** black → one gold point of light ignites → light spreads → content resolves. The lamp is lit.
- **Self-drawing kolam:** dividers, ornaments, and illustrations draw themselves along a continuous path on scroll-into-view.
- **Scroll parallax = stage depth:** foreground performers, mid-ground light, background silk move at different rates to create the depth of a real stage. Restrained (max ~15% differential) — elegant, never dizzying.
- **Tala-pulse micro-motion:** subtle rhythmic "breathing" on ambient elements (a soft 3s or 7s pulse on the logo glow, particle drift) — the site is *alive*, gently keeping time.
- **Abhinaya reveals:** text and images enter the way a dancer reveals emotion — a held beat, then bloom. Stagger children with musical timing (each item a "beat" apart, ~80–120ms).
- **Page transitions:** a warm light-wipe or a curtain of silk/particles; or a kolam that draws across and dissolves. Never a hard cut, never a generic slide.

**Motion hierarchy**
1. **Soloist motion** — the one hero element per view gets the richest, longest, most expressive animation.
2. **Ensemble motion** — supporting elements enter in disciplined stagger, quieter.
3. **Ambient motion** — particles, glow-pulse, silk sheen; continuous, low-amplitude, never demanding attention.
Only one soloist animation runs at a time. Ambient always yields to soloist.

**Micro-interactions**
- Buttons: gold border "ignites" (fills with light L→R) on hover; a faint ghungroo/plucked-string audio cue (opt-in, off by default).
- Links: an underline draws itself like a single kolam stroke.
- Cursor (desktop, optional): a soft gold light-mote the interface reacts to, as if the visitor carries a small lamp.
- Active nav: the current swara glyph glows and softly pulses in its section's key color.

**Performance discipline**
- 60fps non-negotiable. Prefer transform/opacity. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` — provide a dignified static/quiet version (the site should still feel reverent without motion). Particles and WebGL degrade gracefully on low-power devices.

**Motion anti-patterns:** bouncy/rubbery spring toys, fast spinny loaders, parallax that induces nausea, everything-animates-at-once chaos, gratuitous 3D barrel rolls, TikTok-speed jump cuts.

## 14 · TEXTURE LANGUAGE

Textures are **felt, not shouted** — mostly low-opacity overlays that give surfaces the memory of a material. One dominant texture per view.

| Texture | Character | Where / Opacity |
|---|---|---|
| **Silk weave (Kanjeevaram)** | Fine directional weave with zari shimmer | Behind hero/feature imagery; sheen animates on scroll. 8–15% |
| **Handmade paper / palm-leaf (ola)** | Warm fibrous grain, deckled edge | Manuscript/legacy sections, quotes. 6–12% |
| **Gold foil / varak** | Hammered, cracked, leaf-edged | Precious highlights only. Full opacity but tiny area. |

## 15 · ICONOGRAPHY

**Style: brass line icons on a mandala grid.**

- **Construction:** Drawn on an 8-point-derived grid whose proportions echo the **ashtadala (8-petal lotus)** and kolam dot-matrix — so icons feel like a family born of the same geometry.
- **Stroke:** 1.5px optical weight, with a subtle **calligraphic terminal** (a slight thick-thin, not perfectly uniform) — the hand is present. Rounded joins, precise cuts.
- **Color:** Saptham Gold default; Ivory in dense UI; Alta Vermillion only for a single active/alert state.
- **Form language:** Geometric and minimal, but derived from authentic sources — a mudra for "gallery," a lotus for "vision," a gopuram for "legacy," a veena silhouette for "music," a ghungroo for "events." Metaphors are *specific and cultural*, never generic (no default hamburger clichés where a mudra could speak).
- **Behavior:** Icons may self-draw on reveal; active icons gain a faint gold glow.
- **Consistency contract:** all icons share weight, corner radius, grid, and optical size. An icon that doesn't look like it came from the same brass workshop is rejected.

**Forbidden:** filled emoji-style icons, multicolor icons, mismatched stroke weights, trendy duotone, literal photographic icons.

## 16 · MUSICAL IDENTITY

The challenge: honor eight instruments without cluttering. **Solution: reduce each to (a) a signature silhouette and (b) a "sound-form" gesture-line.** Instruments appear as elegant line-art, silhouettes, audio-reactive waveforms, and section markers — never as busy realistic clip-art crowding the UI.

| Instrument | Signature form | Visual gesture | Role in system |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Veena** | The curved *yali* (dragon) head, long fretted neck, round gourd resonator | A long sweeping calligraphic line ending in the yali curl | The *melodic emblem* — most iconic; use as a hero line-motif and for "Carnatic music" wayfinding |
| **Violin (Carnatic)** | Seated-posture silhouette, the scroll head | A tight, expressive curved line (gamaka slides) | "Melody/strings" marker; pairs with veena |
| **Flute (Venu)** | A single horizontal line pierced by finger-hole dots, breath-particles at the end | One clean line + drifting breath motes | *Air/breath* motif — used in calm, spacious sections; its line can literally become a divider |
| **Mridangam** | Twin-circle barrel drum (two faces) | Two concentric pulse-rings (bass + treble) that ripple on beat | The *rhythm emblem* — its two circles drive the tala-pulse motion system |
| **Tambura** | Long slender drone body, four tuning pegs | Not an icon but an *ambient continuous line/gradient* — the eternal sruti | **The site's drone:** represented as the ever-present low background hum/gradient/baseline. The tambura is the whole site's foundation, not a marker. |
| **Nadaswaram** | Long conical pipe flaring at the bell | A flaring, radiant burst-line | *Ceremonial/celebration* marker — used for grand events, arangetrams, finales |
| **Ghatam** | The clay-pot circle, mouth-opening | A warm terracotta circle with a subtle earthen texture | *Earthy rhythm* accent; the humble clay counterpoint to brass |
| **Kanjira** | Small frame-drum circle with a single jingle slit | A small ring with one shimmer-spark | *Small-percussion* accent; the "spark" — pairs with Alta Vermillion moments |

**System rules**
- **Audio-reactive option:** where sound plays, instrument sound-forms may become live waveforms — the veena's line vibrating, the mridangam's rings pulsing. Opt-in, restrained.
- **Never** render all instruments together as a cluttered band. At most one or two per view, as line/silhouette.
- **The tambura is conceptual, not iconographic** — it is the ambient drone (background gradient + optional real audio bed) that underlies the entire experience, exactly as it underlies every raga.

## 17 · DANCE IDENTITY (BHARATANATYAM)

Bharatanatyam supplies the site's **gesture, geometry, and emotion**. Draw from it precisely and respectfully.

| Element | Visual translation |
|---|---|
| **Mudras (hand gestures)** | The primary human-motif icon set. *Pataka* (flag), *Alapadma* (the blooming lotus palm — our "reveal/open" gesture), *Anjali* (namaste — greeting/thank-you moments), *Kartari Mukha*, *Mrigashirsha*. Rendered as gold line-art; used for iconography and hero illustration. The Alapadma is the signature "opening" motif for reveals. |
| **Araimandi (the half-sitting stance)** | The grounded equilateral **triangle/diamond geometry** of the pose becomes a structural motif — the "planted," balanced feeling of layout blocks and the stable base of compositions. |
| **Tribhanga (three-bend posture)** | The elegant S-curve informs our calligraphic line quality and the flow of illustrated dancers. |
| **Costume** | The **Kanjeevaram silk** (jewel tones + zari) drives the fabric textures and jewel palette. The pleated **fan (pambara)** at the front becomes a *radial/pleated motif* for ornaments and loaders. Pleats = rhythm made visible. |
| **Jewelry (temple aabharanam)** | *Nethichutti* (head), *jimikki/jhumka* (ears), *oddiyanam* (waist belt), *maatal*, nose-ring. These inform the **gold detailing** — small, precious, catching light. Jewelry = where our gold-leaf and specular highlights live. |
| **Alta (red dye on hands/feet)** | The source of **Alta Vermillion** — the brightest interactive spark. The red-tipped fingers/feet justify our single hottest accent. |
| **Salangai / Ghungroo (ankle bells)** | Sound + rhythm made visible. Micro-interaction sound cues; a shimmer motif; the "quickening" of the Thillana finale. |
| **Abhinaya (facial expression) & the eyes** | *The eyes lead.* Photography focuses on expressive kohl-lined eyes. Interaction concept: the interface subtly "watches"/responds to the cursor as the dancer's eyes lead the movement. Emotion over spectacle. |
| **Tala (rhythm)** | Governs the spacing scale (§6) and the motion tempo (§13). The dance's rhythmic cycle is literally our timing system. |
| **Navarasa (nine emotions)** | The tonal range of the whole experience — each movement targets a rasa (mapped in §4). |

**Respect clause:** abstract and honor — never trivialize. No cartoon dancers, no "Bollywood-ized" glamour, no deity caricature. When in doubt, use silhouette and line, and let the *real photography* carry the human truth.

## 18 · SACRED GEOMETRY

Geometry is used as **structure and wayfinding**, drawn in gold line, generative and subtle — never wallpaper.

| Motif | Source | Tasteful use |
|---|---|---|
| **Kolam (pulli kolam)** | The dot-grid with a continuous unbroken looping line | **The site's underlying logic.** A faint dot-matrix can be the literal background grid; dividers and loaders are self-drawing kolam strokes; generative kolam patterns mark movement transitions. This is the #1 signature device. |
| **Rangoli** | Radial, symmetrical, colored floor art | Rare focal *ceremonial ornaments* (invocation, festival announcements). Here color is permitted — a single controlled rangoli burst — but used once, as a jewel. |
| **Mandala** | Concentric radial symmetry | Loaders, section-threshold transitions, avatar/portrait frames, the "kindling" load animation radiating outward. |
| **Temple architecture** | Gopuram tiering, vimana, pillared mandapam | **Vertical hierarchy & wayfinding:** the gopuram's stacked tiers map to scroll milestones/timeline; the colonnade/mandapam informs grid and gallery layouts (rhythmic columns like temple pillars); the pointed arch frames key imagery. |
| **Lotus geometry** | Ashtadala (8-petal), golden-ratio petal arrangement | Radial ornaments, the "vision" motif, reveal blooms (Alapadma). Proportioned to phi where possible. |
| **South Indian motifs** | *Yali* (guardian), *hamsa* (swan), peacock, mango/paisley, *bhairavi* border patterns | Fine gold line accents, border ornaments at thresholds, illustrative flourishes. Used sparingly as punctuation, not paragraphs. |

**Geometry discipline**
- Monochrome gold line (or single swara tint). Generative and animated (self-drawing) beats static ornament.
- Geometry earns its place by *doing a job* — structuring, guiding, transitioning — not by filling emptiness. Empty is good (§5).
- Symmetry is reserved for sacred/ceremonial moments (invocation, loaders, blessings); editorial content stays asymmetric and cinematic.
